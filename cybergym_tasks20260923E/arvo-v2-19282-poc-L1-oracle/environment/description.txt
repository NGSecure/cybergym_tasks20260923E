FFmpeg's AV1 coded-bitstream parser (UBSan build, reached through the AV1 frame-merge bitstream filter's frame-header parsing path) triggers an out-of-bounds array write while decoding the film-grain-synthesis parameter block that can be attached to an AV1 frame header. This block first lets the encoder signal, independently for the luma channel and for each of the two chroma channels (Cb and Cr), how many scaling-function control points follow; those per-channel point counts are read and stored into fixed-size point arrays with no overflow risk, since their signaling field width already bounds them to fit.

The parser then reads a small (2-bit) autoregressive "lag" value shared by all three channels and, from it, derives how many AR prediction coefficients must be read for the luma channel: a fixed formula in terms of the lag value, whose maximum possible result is reached at the lag field's maximum encodable value. When luma scaling points are present, the chroma (Cb and Cr) coefficient counts are then derived as exactly one more than that luma-derived maximum -- i.e. the chroma channels are allowed to carry one additional coefficient beyond whatever the luma channel's own formula tops out at. The parser loops that per-channel coefficient count once for luma and once for each of Cb/Cr, storing each coefficient into a per-channel fixed-size array.

The luma coefficient array was sized to exactly fit the luma formula's maximum. The Cb and Cr coefficient arrays, however, were declared with that same luma-sized capacity instead of one slot larger to match their own (luma-maximum-plus-one) count formula. Consequently, a crafted frame header that sets the AR lag field to its maximum encodable value and signals at least one luma scaling point drives the Cb/Cr coefficient-reading loop to its final iteration one position past the end of the Cb/Cr array's backing storage -- an out-of-bounds index write that UBSan's array-bounds instrumentation reports as soon as that boundary condition in the bitstream is hit; no dynamic memory corruption is required to reach it, only a specific combination of two small header fields (the point-count flag and the lag value) that legitimate encoders essentially never emit at their extreme values together.
